## Research selection

**Selected for specialist review.** For the eligible negative prime twists of $y^2=x^3-28x+1$, the sign at a single quartic root determines when the Cassels–Tate pairing is nondegenerate. The negative sign forces rational rank zero and a 2-primary Tate–Shafarevich group of type (2,2). The revised note also proves that a positive-density subset of these primes has nonzero complex central value. The case-specific steps are a parity relation for real modular symbols, a certified value at 31, and simultaneous control of the analytic coefficient and pairing sign. The general BSD rank equality and the priority of this specialization remain open for review. **Fourier residues and rational chords.** For a plane cubic representing any elliptic curve over the rationals, a prime-power Fourier sum selects the rational chords, with exact weights for tangents and flexes. Counting the weighted directions recovers the Mordell–Weil rank. Taking the multiples of one direction recovers the order of the original elliptic L-function by analytic continuation. An appendix combines the real integral with the factors at every prime on a pencil through a nonflex rational point. The resulting boundary coefficient grows with exponent half the rational rank and has no meromorphic extension at zero in odd rank. A second appendix identifies the positive squared Fourier distribution on tangent directions at every finite precision and quantifies the loss from treating rational reductions at different primes as independent. The equality of the two rank measurements and the priority of the specialization remain open. **Splitting genus-one torsors and rank growth.** Let $E$ be an elliptic curve over a totally real number field $K$, with irreducible $E[2]$. If a locally soluble torsor has exact period $2^k$ with $k\geq 1$, there is a tower of $k$ totally real quadratic extensions that splits it, with prescribed splitting at any finite set of finite places. The rank increases at every step. In the normal closure $L$, the bound is $\operatorname{rank} E(L)-\operatorname{rank} E(K)\geq 2^k-1$. This result concerns rank after base extension. ## Drafts

Of the six drafts below, the strongest candidate for external review on BSD-related analysis is the quadratic-character average. Its claimed contribution is the explicit factorization of the average indexed by values of a binary quartic. Counting rational points by their canonical heights is established mathematics. The source chat reports that the full interval certificate needed to apply the heat-sign result to curve 234446.a1 was not recovered. All six are internal working drafts.
